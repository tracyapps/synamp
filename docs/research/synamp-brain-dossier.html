Chapter 01

The shape of the problem

This dossier compiles what the research literature actually supports about music in relation to mood, focus, memory, clarity, and emotion — and then converts that into a concrete design for SynAmp's brain: the layer that scans your own library and turns a natural-language prompt into an accurate, adaptive playlist. Its conclusion is narrow and deliberate. A system that works does not classify artists or songs; it models the recording, stores a wide multi-signal vector per track, compiles language into a typed query object, and treats learning as an explainable re-ranker long before it treats it as statistics. That design is a candidate; the methods underneath it are observed.

The request, restated

Two requests arrived together. The first is a research compilation: what music does to mood, focus, memory, clarity, and emotion. The second matters more. You want to know which metrics can actually be scanned from a personal digital audio library, so that a listener can write an ordinary sentence and receive a playlist that is accurate and that adapts over time. You supplied the failure cases yourself, and they are the specification: every one of them is a case where the tags on a file are silent about the thing you actually mean. The eight are below, unchanged in spirit.

The design thesis, in one page

Model the recording, not the artist.
The unit of similarity is the track's sound, not the name attached to it. Everything else follows from this.
Store a wide multi-signal vector per track.
Objective DSP features, learned embeddings, production and era features, instrument and vocal detectors, and lyric or topic embeddings — held side by side, because no single signal spans all eight cases. This extends the existing AnalysisResult rather than replacing it.
Compile natural language into a typed query object.
Hard filters are separated from soft boosts. Negation becomes an explicit per-track exclusion, never negated text handed to an embedding — that rule is the one part of this design with direct experimental backing.
Playlists are saved queries, re-resolved live.
A playlist is not a frozen list of tracks; it is a query over a versioned store. Add a track, re-scan a model, and the playlist updates itself.
Learning starts as an explainable re-ranker.
Before anything statistical is trained, feedback becomes boosts and penalties you can read back in plain language. Trust first; fitting later.

Chapter 02

What the science actually supports

SynAmp promises to turn one typed sentence — “something to write to, nothing too slow, no words” — into fitting music. That promise rests on psychology holding real findings, real disagreements, and several well-marketed non-findings. This chapter condenses what a designer can build on, and where the honest answer is “choose the mechanism, not the slogan”; evidence states are carried at the strength the research pass gave them.

Music triggers emotion; it does not carry it

The organising account is Juslin’s multi-mechanism framework, BRECVEMA: music evokes emotion through eight partly independent routes — brainstem reflex, rhythmic entrainment, evaluative conditioning, contagion, visual imagery, episodic memory, musical expectancy, and aesthetic judgement (Juslin 2013, PubMed 23769678). reproduced Emotion is a property of a musical event — music plus this listener plus this context, not a single “mood dial”.

The Geneva Emotional Music Scale gives nine music-specific dimensions in three second-order factors (Zentner, Grandjean & Scherer 2008; source unresolved in this pass). reproduced

Most Music Emotion Recognition instead uses two-dimensional valence × arousal, which cannot express the sublimity region (Music Emotion Recognition meta-analysis 2014–2024, Durham repository). reproduced

Perceived ≠ felt. Listeners perceive an expression (“this sounds sad”) without feeling it (Gabrielsson 2002, Musicae Scientiae; source unresolved in this pass). reproduced Mood-tag classifiers measure perceived expression, while “focus” is a felt goal — any direct tag→goal mapping is candidate, learned from feedback.

Expectation theory adds that emotion depends on surprise relative to expectation (Huron’s ITPRA). candidate A track’s value is thus partly a function of familiarity and surprise, not a fixed label. Together: a static per-track mood tag is a lossy projection of a listener-relative phenomenon — usable as a prior, but the felt goal must be closed by feedback.

Tempo, entrainment, and the perceptual centre

Adults’ preferred beat rate is roughly 500 ms — about 120 BPM — close to the modal beat rate of music and to spontaneous tapping (Fraisse 1982; Moelants 2002; source unresolved in this pass). reproduced 120 BPM is the perceptual centre: far below reads as slow and relaxing — the failure your prompt excludes — far above can read as agitated. Tempo is also a pacing lever: the tempo of background music influences the tempo of the activity performed while listening (Kämpfe, Sedlmeier & Renkewitz 2011, ERIC EJ944201). reproduced

Rhythmic entrainment follows an inverted-U: moderate syncopation maximises the urge to move (“groove”), while too little or too much both reduce it (Witek et al. 2014, “Syncopation, Body-Movement and Pleasure in Groove Music”, PLOS ONE; source unresolved in this pass). observed A focus preset belongs in a moderate pulse-clarity, mild-syncopation band — not at either extreme.

Music while you work: the focus case

Under the arousal–mood hypothesis, music affects task performance mainly by shifting arousal and mood, not through musical-cognitive content (Thompson, Schellenberg & Husain 2001, Psychological Science 12(3):248–251; source unresolved in this pass). reproduced If the right music depends on your current arousal, no fixed playlist is optimal — which is the direct motivation for the feedback loop.

A meta-analysis finds a near-null global effect of background music on adult cognition, but real task-specific effects: it disturbs reading, slightly harms memory, and helps emotional reactions and sports achievement (Kämpfe et al. 2011, ERIC EJ944201). reproduced There is no single “focus music”; route by task type.

The meta-analyses disagree in sign — and that is the point. A separate meta-analysis of background music and learning (47 studies) reports a small positive mean effect, around 0.3, rising to medium when music is played before the assessment (de la Mora Velasco et al. 2023, Psychology of Music). reproduced The coefficient is published to three decimals (d = 0.314), but that precision was not verifiable from the accessible abstract, so “around 0.3” is the honest figure.

This positive result sits opposite the near-null/negative one above — exactly what the heterogeneity of tasks and stimuli predicts. No sign may be hard-coded. SynAmp must not ship a rule that background music helps — or harms.

During sustained attention, the cleanest study found that preferred background music raised task-focus by cutting mind-wandering, without significantly reducing reaction time versus silence (Kiss & Linnell 2020/21, Psychological Research 85). observed (n = 40.) The defensible benefit is anti-boredom on low-demand work, not speed.

The best-supported lever is lyrics. Vocal music impairs verbal work where instrumental music does not (Alley & Greene 2008), while high lyrical or rhythmic complexity interferes with serial recall (Perham & Currie 2014, Applied Cognitive Psychology 28(2):279–284; source unresolved in this pass). The mechanism is the irrelevant-speech / changing-state effect: task-irrelevant, speech-like sound competes with verbal short-term memory (Colle & Welsh 1976; Salamé & Baddeley 1982). reproduced “No words” is the most actionable rule available.

The arousal curve completes the picture. Lapses split into mind-wandering (arousal too low) and external distraction (too high); a direct comparison found more felt cognitive effort under high-arousing music on a demanding attentional task (Gigliotti et al. 2025, Cognitive Research 2025). observed A focus preset should sit at mid-arousal, not maximal, and the optimum should shift down as task demand rises.

The claim that “video-game music helps you focus” has no strong direct experimental support here; treat it as a popular claim resting only on separately supported properties — instrumental, high pulse clarity, moderately predictable.

“Clarity” is colloquial, not a construct

Mental clarity has no validated audio measurement. unverified It is shorthand for a bundle that is definable: arousal optimisation (the inverted-U above), low distraction (absence of changing-state or speech-like audio), predictability (low surprise, stable pulse, slow harmonic change), low lyrical presence, and low personal salience. The “attention drainage” framing rests on single-study evidence (Chou 2010, ERIC EJ882124). observed Clarity is measurable only through proxies, validated only through feedback.

Memory: a capture risk for focus, a deliberate aid for encoding

Music can cue autobiographical memories. In Janata, Tomic and Rakowski's 2007 study, about 30% of song presentations in that study evoked memories (Memory, 2007 — abstract). Janata's 2009 imaging study examined the neural response to autobiographically salient music (Cerebral Cortex, 2009 — full-text page). observed

Attribution corrected 2026-10-07 from fast-brain research A1/MP-29 and a fresh check of indexed primary-source text (direct page opening was blocked): the 2009 study's 30% threshold selected participants from an earlier validation study; it was not a general finding that 30% of songs evoke memories. The 2007 result is specific to that study, and neither study proves that familiar music impairs focus in this listener.

Earworms (involuntary musical imagery) are near-universal by lifetime prevalence — reported by more than 90% of people surveyed (Dodds et al. 2025, Consciousness and Cognition 130:103834, ScienceDirect). reproduced

But the same literature reports 29.2% of a 2025 sample experienced earworms never. “>90%” and “29.2% never” cannot both describe one lifetime quantity: prevalence is reproduced in direction, unresolved in magnitude. That earworms impair concurrent verbal work is a candidate argument, not established.

Separately, music can serve as a mnemonic: sung material can be better remembered than spoken, partly through slower articulation (Kilgour, Jakobson & Cuddy 2000; source unresolved in this pass). observed But this is a deliberate encoding technique, not a background-listening benefit, and must not be conflated with “music helps me focus”.

What not to implement

  • Mozart-specific presets. A meta-analysis found little evidence of a specific performance-enhancing Mozart effect (Pietschnig, Voracek & Formann 2010, Intelligence 38(3)); a later review found no Mozart-specific mechanism, with any small effects indistinguishable from “any other music” (Oberleiter et al. 2023, Scientific Reports). observed
  • Binaural beats. Evidence is inconsistent: a review of 14 studies found 5 supportive and 8 contradictory for the entrainment hypothesis (Ingendoh et al. 2023, PLOS ONE), conflicting with an earlier favourable meta-analysis (Garcia-Argibay et al. 2019, PubMed 30073406). candidate at best.
  • Isochronic tones. Marketed as entrainment; no adequate replicated evidence located. unverified
  • 432 Hz tuning as a quality or healing claim. No credible peer-reviewed support located in this pass. unverified At most a preference tag.
  • “Music raises IQ.” Disproven as stated: mere listening does not raise general intelligence. reproduced (as a non-finding).

From construct to computable metric

Every construct SynAmp cares about is under-determined by audio, so the system must carry an explicit proxy for each, plus the assumption that the proxy stands in for the construct. This is the bridge into Chapter 3. Every proxy below is candidate until feedback confirms it.

Nothing here has been run against your library; effect sizes are reported only at the precision the sources supported.

Chapter 03

The scanable metric taxonomy: what a library can actually yield

Your complaint is not that bpm, key, energy, danceability, mood, instrumental and instruments{} are wrong. It is that they are too coarse to compile a sentence into. Key is not mood. BPM is not energy. Year is not era. And nothing in the current AnalysisResult can exclude a track by the way it sounds.

What follows is a taxonomy of metrics computable automatically from your ~45,700 local files, organised so each one maps onto a phrase you would actually type. Read the evidence states precisely: the methods are observed — a documented library or paper does what is described — but nothing is confirmed on your library, because the analyzer has no models wired yet. Every per-library accuracy claim is candidate.

The ten families

Rhythm and timing

BPM is the weakest signal here. Tempo is a tempogram peak over the onset-strength envelope, but octave errors near 60/120/240 need beat strength and event density to disambiguate. Pulse clarity and microtiming are the load-bearing members, treated below.

Tonality and harmony

Absolute key is a weak mood signal; mode, chord-change behaviour and dissonance carry the affect. Key is chroma matched to a profile (Krumhansl–Schmuckler, Temperley, EDMA); chord progression comes from a deep-chroma chord network. Mainstream chord models are major/minor only, and degrade on dense mixes.

Timbre and spectrum

Mostly internal proxies rather than labels. MFCCs carry the timbral envelope; flatness separates noise-like from tone-like. No open "acousticness" metric exists — the commercial one is undisclosed — so use a flatness/MFCC/centroid/percussiveness proxy.

Loudness and dynamics

Mastering practice is a cheap fingerprint of when a record was made. Integrated loudness and loudness range come per ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128; crest factor is peak minus RMS; clipping density counts near-0 dBFS samples. Together they are the compression cue behind the era story.

Vocal and instrumental

There is no open equivalent of the proprietary instrumentalness catch-all; the honest substitute is a singing-voice detector. A frame-level classifier collapses to a track-level vocal fraction. Soft, rapped and chopped-sample vocals confuse it, so thresholds are mandatory.

Instrumentation

Multi-label instrument detection is what makes "no piano" excludable: a pretrained CNN over log-mel yields per-instrument confidences. Stem energies give mix balance; the harmonic/percussive ratio separates a tonal bed from a rhythmic one. Note: sax has no confirmed producer among the named instrument models.

Structure

Structure tells you where a track changes, not what the sections mean: boundaries from a self-similarity matrix plus novelty-curve peak-picking, repetition from SSM coverage. This family feeds "has a build-up", not a filter.

High-level semantic

The closest thing to mood, and learned and approximate — never ground truth. Multi-label classifiers yield relaxing, dark, epic, party; valence/arousal regression gives the axes behind "nothing too relaxing". Composite energy should fuse loudness, flux, tempo, roughness and onset rate. Meditativeness has no standard descriptor.

Style-texture proxies

To exclude punk or country by sound you need texture, not tags. A distortion index combines flatness, high-order harmonics and low crest; a country proxy adds acoustic share, nasal formants and diatonic harmony; a punk proxy adds fast tempo and a compressed master. Expect false negatives.

Learned embeddings

An audio embedding is a metric in its own right: a dense vector where distance means "how alike do these sound". It underpins "more like this" and the whole adaptive-learning loop. A joint text–audio space ranks free text against audio. The embedder choice is a licence decision — Chapter 5 owns it.

Metrics that beat metadata

Five items do work no tag in AnalysisResult can do. They are the point of this chapter.

Pulse clarity
Shannon entropy of the fluctuation spectrum — the autocorrelation of the onset-strength envelope across metrical lags; low entropy means an unambiguous pulse. The definition traces to Lartillot et al. 2008; the second attribution in the source material could not be verified and is not relied on candidate. This separates groovable from floating, which BPM cannot do: a 90 BPM ambient drift and a 90 BPM funk groove share their tempo.
Swing and microtiming deviation, with a signed lead/lag trend
Three quantities over a beat grid: swing (the ratio of the two eighth-note subdivisions in a beat — 0.5 straight, about 0.667 triplet), tightness (mean absolute onset deviation from the grid), and a signed trend in that deviation — ahead of the grid reads urgent, behind reads laid-back. The method is published, but no one-call audio swing detector exists, so it must be built on a beat tracker candidate. It is your energy hypothesis, and the highest-value unbuilt metric here.
Mode, chord-change behaviour and dissonance
These, not the tonic, are the real mood carriers: mode is the most reliable tonal affect cue, chord-change rate and harmonic complexity describe how the harmony moves, and dissonance measures roughness between neighbouring partials. The schema consequence is a demotion — keep key, but let a chord-progression embedding, mode, change rate and dissonance drive the affect score observed.
Production and mastering characteristics
Integrated loudness, loudness range, crest factor and clipping density form a cheap era fingerprint: high LUFS with a narrow range, a low crest factor and heavy clipping reads as a modern hyper-compressed master; low LUFS with a wide range reads as older or audiophile. The direction is observed as an aggregate trend across recordings. No validated per-track decade classifier exists, though, so per-track era discrimination stays candidate and must not be sold as a classifier.
Audio embeddings
The whole-similarity substrate: a dense vector where cosine distance answers "how alike do these sound", independent of tag, artist or year. Every "more like this" request and the whole adaptive-learning loop run on it. Its weakness is the black box: pair it with the explainable features above.

On cost. Every cost figure in the research corpus is an unmeasured estimate on a single core, and the source's own cost model is internally inconsistent — its per-tool rows sum far above its headline total. Do not budget from any figure here; Chapter 8 recomputes it. One structural point survives: loudness, tempo and spectral features may be computed on a short sample window, but loudness range, crest factor, the era cue, segmentation and microtiming may not be, because truncation removes exactly what they measure.

Prompts to predicates

The compilation target is a predicate set over the fields above. Thresholds are starting guesses to be tuned, not calibrated values.

Not reliably computable

Stated plainly:

  • Subject, theme or topic. Needs lyrics plus topic modelling or captioning; not an audio-DSP property, and structurally absent from the instrumental subset.
  • Vocal gender. Ranges overlap heavily and falsetto, androgynous and processed voices break the cue. At most a soft, low-weight preference with a disclaimer — never a hard filter.
  • Crisp "punkiness" or "countryness". Only weak composite proxies exist; expect imperfect exclusion.
  • Modality beyond major/minor (Dorian, Aeolian, modulation counting). Research-grade and unreliable.
  • "Focus", "meditative", "emotional" as first-class labels. No standard descriptor — only proxy bundles of tempo, pulse clarity, vocal fraction, dynamics and arousal.
  • Genre tags. Noisy, culture-dependent, and exactly what you distrust. At best one weak hint.
  • Exact instrument presence in dense mixes. Classifiers and separation both bleed; per-instrument claims stay probabilistic and thresholded.
  • Absolute era from the year tag. The failure you named; it must be replaced by the sound-based cue bundle, itself only candidate.

One boundary to carry forward: this taxonomy specifies the fields, but the runtime query object in the source material was built independently and shares almost none of them. Reconciling the two is the central work of Chapter 6.

Chapter 04

The recording, not the artist: the Ani DiFranco problem

You asked for more tracks that sound like these two Ani DiFranco recordings; what came back was, in effect, more Ani DiFranco. Not the same request, and the gap between them is this chapter: the failure stated precisely, why file, artist, genre and year data cannot close it, and the mechanism that can — a model of the recording plus an explicit sonic-period label, with artist demoted to a guard and never the ranking signal.

The failure, stated precisely

The perceptual unit is the recording — not the artist, not the album, not the genre, not the year. Artist, album, genre and year are properties of a release and its marketing category; “sounds like” is a property of a signal. Every aggregation over tags answers a question about the release, not the sound. candidate Your premise — that adjacent Ani DiFranco albums differ drastically — is treated here as a requirement, not a verified musicological claim; no citable source for her specific sonic periods was located in this pass. unverified

Why metadata fails, and why raw audio similarity only half-works

The measured reason is stronger than “tags are coarse”: audio similarity carries a strong album-and-artist bias. Flexer & Schnitzer found that album and artist filters materially change similarity behaviour: same-session, same-production material forms dense clumps in feature space (Flexer & Schnitzer 2010, Computer Music Journal). observed That one effect cuts both ways.

So an artist must be represented as a set of period centroids — never one vector or one tag. Two supporting observations: audio features carry enough style-and-time structure to date and segment popular-music history (Mauch et al. 2015, Royal Society Open Science); and content-based features are the only signals that generalise to a novel or unpopular item (McFee et al. 2011, arXiv:1105.2344). Play counts and collaborative filtering do not exist in a self-hosted library. observed

The proposed mechanism

An ordered recipe; every step is a SynAmp design proposal, none run on your library. candidate

  1. Per-track record. Store an audio embedding, a production vector, a rhythm vector, a chroma/chord vector, a lyric vector, and identity and version flags (fingerprint, recording ID, album ID, catalogue year, sonic era; flags for live, cover, remaster, duplicate cluster).
  2. Album aggregation. Reduce each album to centroids of the audio and production vectors plus a within-album spread — an album is a production-consistent recording session.
  3. Period clustering. Cluster album centroids per artist (agglomerative over cosine distance, or a hidden Markov model over release order) into periods P1…Pn. A period break is a large centroid jump between consecutive releases.
  4. Period as a first-class filter. A stored label makes artist:Ani_DiFranco AND sonic_period:P1 expressible — “this Ani, not that Ani”, which is exactly what “more by this artist” cannot say.
  5. Intentional era-spanning. Because periods are per-recording labels, “span her whole arc” becomes “sample at least one track from each period, ordered by period distance”, not “return everything by the artist” — and it needs a per-period coverage floor so thin periods are not silently skipped (see Chapter 6).

Multi-exemplar query design

“Sounds like these N recordings” is a centroid in a learned metric space, and its accuracy depends on the metric being learned rather than on raw features (McFee et al. 2011). Classical relevance feedback supports the shape: a Rocchio-style query combines the original query, the mean of relevant items, and a negatively-weighted mean of non-relevant items (Manning, Raghavan & Schütze, ch. 9, PDF, observed). SynAmp departs from textbook Rocchio in six ways. candidate

Multiple prototypes, not one centroid
Pin one early and one late song and a single average lands between periods, matching neither. Choose k by clustering the exemplars (silhouette over k ∈ {1,2,3}), issue k queries, round-robin the results. This is the most important guard for your case; low silhouette is the detection signal.
Pinned versus inferred weighting
User-pinned exemplars default to 1.0; auto-added album-mates get decayed weights. Keep the anchor (never crossed) versus soft seed distinction explicit in the interface.
Robust centroid estimation
A trimmed or medoid-style estimator, so one idiosyncratic exemplar cannot drag the query out of the period.
Multi-view fusion
Score = wa·cos(audio) + wp·cos(production) + wt·cos(text) + wc·cos(chord), starting near (0.5, 0.3, 0.1, 0.1) and learned from feedback. The production weight wp does the era discrimination; the text weight stays capped, because vague text drifts to hubs.
Anti-exemplars are a filter, not a subtraction
Mean-centroid subtraction in high dimensions overshoots into directions that correspond to no real music. Prefer hard-negative rejection — drop candidates whose similarity to the anti-set exceeds sim(positive) − margin — with a small subtracted term only as a tie-breaker.
Same-artist era guard
If a candidate’s artist equals the exemplar’s artist, require its album centroid (or production distance) within τ, else downweight by exp(−d). This stops “more by Ani” infection while admitting correct-period tracks.

Version and era handling

Three independent identities must be tracked separately; conflating them is what makes era queries fail.

The four-tier near-duplicate policy follows: (a) exact fingerprint match → duplicate; (b) fingerprint plus high embedding cosine → same master, different encode; (c) composition match, low embedding cosine → cover or version; (d) high embedding cosine, no fingerprint and no composition match → sonic near-duplicate, e.g. two takes from one session. This also gives playlists the collision-safe “collapse duplicates to one representative” behaviour they need.

A cover from another era is legitimate. A cover is a new sonic object: it belongs to the cover’s own era and production while sharing composition with the original. So “same song” queries follow composition identity and “sounds like” queries follow sonic character; when the two disagree, SynAmp must link and warn. It must explain “same song, different era” rather than silently “correct” the result toward the year tag. candidate

Store both era notions side by side: the catalogue year (the year tag — free, but a release date, poisoned by remasters, compilations and reissues) and a sonic-era estimate measured from the signal, the only one that survives a modern cover of an old song. Loudness and dynamics have measurably changed across decades of production (loudness-war literature; source unresolved in this pass). Flag the conflict: a track whose sonic era is decades later than its year tag is where the year tag lies.

Hubness

In high-dimensional space a few tracks become universal neighbours and every query drifts toward them; these hubs have lower perceptual similarity to their neighbours than non-hubs do (Flexer, Schnitzer & Schlüter, ISMIR 2012). observed Mutual-proximity or hub-scaling correction applied before ranking was measured to decisively improve hub-dominated audio similarity. Without it, every query returns the same handful of songs.

Failure modes

Three corrections applied to this chapter’s source

1. The embedder cannot ship as specified. The source recommends MERT or MuQ as the audio embedder. Their weights are non-commercial (MERT weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0; MuQ comparable), so they can be a private, non-distributed accuracy option only. observed The shippable backbone is a permissive general audio embedder — OpenL3 — paired with a text–audio model, MS-CLAP, for coarse user-worded steering. Chapter 5 costs that trade: the licence-clean path is the accuracy-weak path, and the accuracy-strong path cannot be distributed.

2. The acceptance test is circular as written. The source proposes holding out a track from period Pk and requiring its period-mates to outrank the artist’s other periods. But the periods are defined by the same vectors used to validate them, so the test is near-tautological. A non-circular test needs an independent era ground truth — and with a single user it may simply not exist. Until then this capability is unverifiable, not merely unproven. unverified

3. The era discriminator is half-specified. The production vector — the component that carries era — depends on stereo width, reverb decay and noise floor, none of which appear in Chapter 3’s taxonomy. Those dimensions need a producer or must be deleted; otherwise the vector the whole mechanism leans on is incomplete. candidate

Evidence summary. Observed: the album/artist bias, hubness, cover-detection precision, the content-similarity motivation, era-from-audio segmentation, cross-cultural similarity variance. Candidate: the sonic-period model, the multi-exemplar query algebra, the four-tier duplicate policy, the same-artist era guard — designed, not run. Unverified: your premise about Ani DiFranco’s adjacent albums as a musicological claim, and the period-label capability wherever no independent era ground truth exists. Nothing here is reproduced or confirmed.

Chapter 05

The toolchain and the licence fork: what can ship, what cannot

You set a second requirement alongside the music: this should eventually be something other people install — a Synology package, not only your private server. That sentence sorts the whole toolchain into two piles. This chapter fixes the rule already in the project, applies it signal by signal, names the components that cannot enter a distributed build, admits the signals with no permissive option, reads the data sources at their real terms, records what the platforms have closed — and ends on the fork this forces, which only you can decide.

The governing rule already in the project

The repo baseline already carries it as decision D6, and it is binding, not a preference: prefer permissively licensed components in anything that ships; keep AGPL and non-commercial components out of the serving path; treat heavy analysis as offline build-time tooling whose numeric outputs — never its code, never its weights — are what ship. observed

Two mechanisms force that shape. AGPL §13 means a network-offered service that is or links an AGPL component must offer its source to users — which bites the moment SynAmp runs as someone else's service. And a non-commercial licence forbids the commercial distribution you are aiming at regardless of copyleft. Keeping heavy analysis offline and private — a batch job whose output is a table of numbers — is the one arrangement that lets you use the strongest tools while shipping none of them.

The distinction most surveys get wrong: the licence of the code is not the licence of the weights

This is the load-bearing correction of the chapter. A repository's LICENSE file governs the code; the checkpoints it downloads are a separate work with a separate licence, and the two routinely disagree. A permissive codebase can ship non-commercial weights; a permissive project can host a non-commercial model family. So “the repo says MIT, we're fine” is not a valid reading, and any audit that stops at the repo header is wrong. observed

The shippable path, signal by signal

The permissive choices — MIT / BSD / ISC / Apache-2.0 / CC0 — that carry each signal SynAmp needs. Every licence was read at source; unverified residue is flagged below.

The doubly constrained components

Three families cannot be in a distributed build under any reading:

  • The all-in-one audio analysis library. Essentia is AGPL-3.0, and its whole house model zoo — Discogs-EffNet embeddings, the MTG-Jamendo mood/theme/instrument heads, Danceability, Voice/instrumental, TempoCNN — is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial and ShareAlike, with only a negotiated proprietary licence otherwise. Code and weights are both blocked. observed
  • The beat-tracking toolkit. madmom's code is BSD, but its models are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — the models, not the code, disqualify it. Use its code paths only, or prefer beat_this. observed
  • The two strongest music-representation models. MERT (weights CC-BY-NC-4.0) and MuQ (comparable) are the best available embedders, and both are private-only. observed

The honest gap list: no permissively licensed option at all

Five signals have no permissive pre-trained producer. Each mitigation exists, and each is the accuracy-weak path.

The gap lands on the hard cases the project exists to solve — mood, era, and the microtiming/danceability energy problem. The permissive path reaches them only with prompting and heuristics, never a trained model.

Data sources and their real terms

  • MusicBrainz — the metadata backbone. Core data is CC0; supplementary fields are CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; server code is GPL-2.0 — use the core freely, treat supplementary as non-commercial (data licence).
  • AcoustID / Chromaprint — the fingerprint service. Chromaprint code is MIT but bundles an LGPL-2.1 FFmpeg portion, so treat the whole as LGPL-2.1. The AcoustID database licence is unverified.
  • AcousticBrainz — the frozen CC0 archive. All data is CC0, but collection stopped in 2022 and the corpus is frozen at roughly 7.56 M unique / 29.5 M total recordings, matchable only by MusicBrainz ID — legally ideal, but frozen coverage rules it out as a sole source (MusicBrainz AcousticBrainz doc).
  • Lyrics services — LRCLIB's server code is MIT, but the contributed lyric content licensing is unverified (lrclib licence); Genius forbids commercial use without a licence (source resolvable only at domain level — unresolved in this pass); Musixmatch is partners-only.
  • Last.fm — community tags and similarity, non-commercial by terms with attribution and no sub-licensing (API ToS). Good offline for you personally; unsafe to ship.
  • Discogs — genre/style and release metadata; monthly dumps are CC0, live API data is restricted, so prefer the dumps (developer terms). Wikidata structured data is CC0 (download).

Flagged unverified rather than asserted: the AcoustID database licence, LRCLIB lyric content, PANNs checkpoint terms, and the MS-CLAP / LAION-CLAP checkpoint and training-data terms. Do not treat any of these as settled.

Closed APIs and deprecations

The industry's ready-made audio-features surface has closed. The big streaming platform's Audio Features and Audio Analysis endpoints were deprecated on 27 November 2024: new apps can no longer obtain them, and only pre-existing extended-mode apps retain access (deprecation blog; the endpoint reference now carries a Deprecated badge). The whole Echo-Nest-style set — tempo, key, energy, danceability, valence, acousticness, instrumentalness — is therefore unavailable to a new app and must be built locally. observed

And the closest commercial analogue to what you are building is a paid third-party service. Cyanite offers genre, mood, instruments, lyric theme, tempo, natural-language descriptions and “match by sound, vibe, era” — era plus natural-language query, the SynAmp brain's shape. It is proprietary and paid (cost unverified in this pass), and using it means uploading your catalogue to a third party — a direct conflict with the self-hosted, private goal. observed

The fork — a decision only you can make

Everything above converges on one choice the research cannot make for you: it trades legality against accuracy, and neither side is free. The licence-clean path is the accuracy-weak path; the accuracy-strong path cannot be distributed.

Chapter 06

The pipeline, reconciled: from a sentence to a playlist

This chapter presents the corrected, whole pipeline from a free-text sentence to an ordered, explainable, self-refreshing playlist. Three documents were written independently and disagreed — the pipeline design, the metric taxonomy and the learning data model each defined a query object, and the shapes did not match. They are merged here: the taxonomy supplies field names, the learning model the persistence shape, the pipeline the mechanics. The mechanics are observed; the assembly is candidate.

The pipeline as ordered stages

  1. Intake and session context. Prompt, library state, recent plays, saved exemplars, locale.
  2. Parse. A local LLM under constrained decoding emits a typed plan; it cannot emit an invalid plan.
  3. Compile. Deterministic and LLM-free: plan in, predicate AST, normalized score terms, structured exclusions and affirmative-only text out.
  4. Candidate generation, two channels. An indexed filter pass (set A); an approximate-nearest-neighbour pass over audio embeddings (set B).
  5. Relaxation check. Under-fill walks a declared ladder over permitted mutations only, each one recorded.
  6. Multi-objective scoring. Normalize each signal, weight it, multiply by a query-type-modulated personalisation term.
  7. Diversity-aware selection. Greedy Maximal Marginal Relevance with hard caps.
  8. Sequencing. Shortest Hamiltonian path through tempo, key and timbre, weighted by an arc template.
  9. Explanation. Per-track reasons mined from the plan's predicates and the track's measured values.
  10. Persistence. Store the plan, not the track list: a saved query with store and index versions attached.
  11. Live refresh. On a new file, re-run candidate generation through sequencing; membership and order are a recomputed view.
  12. Feedback capture. Love, remove, skip and replay keyed to plan, track and position, handed to Chapter 7.

Two stages, and the anti-pattern

Candidate generation followed by a learned re-ranker is the industrial shape, and why this pipeline is staged rather than scored in one pass. The supporting study builds candidates with an LLM embedder, re-ranks with a collaborative model, and fuses the two multiplicatively with a query-type classifier driving the exponent (NLP4MusA 2026). That is one paper on a 5,000-query streaming corpus with a collaborative backend, so call the pattern candidate.

The anti-pattern that source documents matters more: never let the LLM emit track titles or identifiers. Generative retrieval over titles needs a word tokenizer, an entity-resolution layer and decode cost linear in title length (Text2Tracks). The model emits a query; retrieval happens in the store — which keeps the plan auditable and makes negation exact.

Three moves in that schema reconcile the two documents. production_vec becomes first-class so sonic era is scored from sound, with year demoted to a low-weight hint; when they disagree the production view wins and the disagreement is logged. A chord view joins text, exemplar, feature, mood, novelty and taste. And period_coverage adds a floor beside max_per_decade, so "span the whole arc" is a constraint rather than an accident.

Negation and exclusion

This doctrine survives adversarial review intact: never encode negation inside an embedding. Text–audio models carry a documented affirmation bias — "P, with no Y" sits as close to "P, with Y" as a paraphrase sits to its original, and the negated query still prefers audio containing the forbidden sound (NegEval-Audio, arXiv 2607.12290) observed. Negated multiple-choice accuracy is reported at 0.2–1.1% against a 25% chance baseline. So "no piano" handed to a text encoder risks raising piano retrieval.

Every negation compiles to an explicit, per-track, testable predicate; negated spans are stripped before any text encoding; the compiler is unit-tested against these pairs.

One gap is now closed: unknown_policy must distinguish "verified not-punk" from "no genre tag at all". An untagged track is not evidence of anything, and treating a missing tag as a pass silently dropped tracks you never excluded. Untagged tracks are counted, surfaced and queued.

Hard, soft, quota

Three categories, not two. Hard is a filter, applied at candidate generation. Soft is a score term with a weight. Quota is a minimum-satisfaction ratio over the final set — where "mostly female artists" belongs, since it is neither a filter (which would zero out collaborations) nor a pure boost (which guarantees nothing); unmet, it re-ranks, then discloses the achieved ratio.

Thresholds are two-sided: every numeric hard predicate records its value and the planner's confidence that it encodes the phrase. Because "energy" is not one number, the plan also records which proxy was used — arousal composite, microtiming deviation, or loudness-and-flux — so a wrong answer can be diagnosed instead of argued about.

Ranking, diversity and sequencing

The score is a normalised weighted sum of the ranking signals, multiplied by a personalisation term raised to a query-type-modulated exponent: semantic first, then personalised, with the exponent near zero for catalogue and exclusion queries. That modulation is candidate here.

Selection is MMR-greedy with the audio embedding as the similarity kernel, plus hard caps on artist, album and period and a floor on embedding spread; a novelty-versus-history term stops "focus" replaying the same twelve tracks. Sequencing is a shortest Hamiltonian path over log-tempo, circle-of-fifths key and timbre; curators preferred sequenced playlists and marked materially fewer abrupt consecutive pairs than random ordering (Bittner et al., ISMIR 2017). "Focus" asks for the flat arc — low variance, not a build.

Explainability

A reason is derived from the plan's predicates and the track's measured values — (source_phrase, field, value, threshold, margin) — rendered by deterministic templates. The LLM may only paraphrase those facts and may never introduce a reason. Every hard rule shows its excluded count, the one thing embedding negation cannot offer, and detailed mode adds position rationale.

Chapter 07

Learning from feedback, for an audience of one

Almost every recommender result comes from multi-user industrial systems — Spotify, YouTube, Yahoo!, WeChat, Netflix — learned from millions to billions of events (Covington et al., YouTube DNN). You are one listener. The methods transfer; the statistical leverage does not: feedback arrives slowly, and every choice below follows from that. observed

Instead you have a server-owned playhead — a skip is a real event — and, for the crowd's missing scale, content features. Tracks share features; users do not.

The feedback taxonomy

Each event is a signed reward with a confidence weight: implicit-feedback factorisation treats data as an “indication of positive and negative preference associated with vastly varying confidence levels” (Hu, Koren & Volinsky, CF for Implicit Feedback). It yields (sign, magnitude, confidence) → r = sign × magnitude, plus a scope. Magnitudes below are candidate.

The third axis is intention, and it is the one most systems omit. A CHI'25 study separates unintentional implicit feedback (consumption) from intentional implicit feedback — acts taken to refine recommendations — finding the latter drives diversity and relevance (Kuo et al., CHI'25, arXiv:2502.09869). observed So remove is intentional and aimed at this playlist; a skip is usually unintentional and context-dependent — Jannach's warning that it “can be context dependent” and misreads as a general negative assessment (Jannach et al., Implicit Feedback).

Early skip is a guess, not a finding. Use an absolute floor (roughly 20–30 s) and a relative one (roughly 25% of duration) so short tracks are not punished. The “first 30 seconds decide everything” framing is commercial-blog lore, source unresolved in this pass: treat it as a tunable starting guess (unverified as a finding). WeChat's dwell reweighting (Xie et al., WWW'23, arXiv:2209.09000) is better supported; SynAmp's dwell is play fraction of duration.

The asymmetry of negatives

Remove is not thumbs-down is not skip. Remove is hard, high-confidence and destructive; it reads best as “never in this playlist again” — a playlist-instance hard negative — not automatically a global dislike. Thumbs-down is weaker but explicit; a skip is neither. Promotion rule: a track-level negative goes global only on corroboration across ≥ 2–3 scopes, or ≥ M occurrences. candidate

Negative weights stay small. In Rocchio feedback, “positive feedback ... turns out to be much more valuable than negative feedback, and so most IR systems set γ < β” (Manning et al., ch. 9, IR Book 09expand). Prefer cheap hard-excludes for remove over large negative gradients; contrastive learning on skips (Seshadri et al., RecSys'23, arXiv:2309.11623) is a later-phase technique.

The identifiability problem. A negative is signal only if you can tell which feature it is about. In SynAmp's composite energy the inputs co-vary — tempo, loudness, onset_rate, spectral_flux rise in lockstep — so one thumbs-down cannot identify the offending proxy. Corroboration reduces noise, not confounding; separating collinear features needs designed exploration far beyond a 10–15% slot budget. Feature-level attribution here is unverified; v1–v2 should learn scope and entity preferences and hold correlated weights provisional.

Cold start with no crowd

There is no collaborative cold start — no “new user in a crowd”. The cold-start state is simply “no feedback yet”:

  1. Prompt intent — the query object (hard constraints, energy and mood targets, era, instrument weights) plus a seed embedding (Chapter 6).
  2. Existing library evidence — play counts, star ratings and existing playlists: priors that exist before any learning.
  3. Content similarity — embeddings retrieve neighbours of the seed set (Hansen et al., CoSeRNN, Spotify Research); the architecture is portable, the weights are not.

Popularity prior: sparingly. Implicit data is long-tailed; over-trusting popularity yields a “blockbuster effect” and “starvation of the niche items” (Jannach et al.). Prefer within-library co-occurrence.

Methods, from cheap to advanced

Deep sequence models are over-engineered here; LLM summaries serve only explanation and cold-start seeding, never the scorer. Bandits fit “when the item set changes quickly ... or when the rate of traffic is low” (Yan, Bandits for Recommender Systems) — but a per-track arm is wrong for one user: sample over shared content features, warm-started from the affinity table. LinUCB was validated on 33M Yahoo! events (Li et al.); feature-sharing transfers, certainty does not.

Scoping: global, query, playlist, session

One gesture, four readings. The UI affordance declares the scope: where the action happened and which control was used is the declaration. observed

All scopes resolve to one structure: an affinity cell keyed by (entity_type, entity_id, scope, scope_id); ranking aggregates all four levels in one pass. candidate

Era correction. The source implies era boundaries are refined by feedback. They are not: era lives in the production vector, and a thumb carries no era label, so no volume of thumbs will teach a period boundary. Period labels come from Chapter 4's clustering and stay unverified without independent ground truth. Feedback re-ranks within a period; it cannot discover one.

Drift, exploration and filter bubbles

Drift. Score each affinity cell as a decayed sum, score = Σ wⁱ · exp(−Δt / τ): a short half-life for session and query cells (days), a long one for global (months). Drift is expected; the model should forget. observed

Exploration. A recommender that influences exposure and then learns from it “perpetuat[es] the feedback loop”, and lack of exploration causes “satiation effect, i.e., reduced enjoyment” (Chen et al., RecSys'21, ACM). Exploration is a designed budget, not noise: reserve a slot share for tracks outside the exploited cluster, optimistic-initialise by feature uncertainty, and penalise near-duplicates. Guardrail: track library coverage as a coverage alarm and raise the budget when it collapses. Diversity, novelty and coverage are guardrails, never objectives — otherwise the system wins its own metrics while making the library worse.

Phased plan

v1 — heuristic, content-first, explainable

Trigger: day 0 to roughly the first 50–200 events. Re-rank with a handful of rules: +artist affinity from a global love, hard-exclude tracks removed from this playlist, penalise tracks skipped twice here, a corroboration-gated global penalty for thumbs_down. Can learn: coarse high-signal preferences — favourite artists, hard bans, obvious exclusions. Cannot learn: feature interactions; the relative weight of more than three signals; drift — and it treats all skips alike.

v2 — embedding preference model with light exploration

Trigger: a few hundred events. Rocchio expansion per query (α = 1, β = 0.75, γ = 0.15); a global taste vector: a decayed centroid of loved and saved exemplars; recency and confidence weighting; light learning-to-rank if enough time-split pairwise labels exist, debiased from the exposure log; an exploration layer over the affinity table. Can learn: multi-feature trade-offs; query-specific constraints; per-scope weights; recency drift. Cannot learn: deep nonlinear interactions reliably — it risks overfitting a few hundred labels.

v3 — learned re-ranker, drift-aware, optional introspection

Trigger: thousands of events, many months. A GBDT re-ranker over the full feature set, gated by counterfactual offline evaluation; a contextual bandit with drift-aware retraining; an optional LLM taste profile for introspection and cold-starting, never the ranker. Can learn: nonlinear interactions; context-dependent preference; budgeted exploration; long-term drift. Cannot learn: a genuinely new taste it never exposes you to — no volume of single-user data escapes the bubble without an explicit diversity objective; and still unverified, attribution among collinear features.

Evaluation with one user

Observational is not interventional. An offline metric answers “how well do recommendations fit logged behaviour”, not “would listening improve if they changed”: static offline evaluation “ignores the interventional nature of recommendations” (Yan, Counterfactual Evaluation). observed

Offline, distrusted. NDCG, Precision@k and Recall@k on time-split (not random) held-out feedback are sanity signals only; rank correlation between predicted score and realised reward is more honest. Counterfactual estimators — IPS, SNIPS, doubly-robust — use the propensity field (Gilotte et al., arXiv:1801.07030).

Online, the ones that matter. Early-skip, completion, save, repeat, remove and love rates, plus playlist survival (reopened after the session); coverage, novelty and diversity are guardrails. A split A/B has almost no power at n = 1: prefer interleaving (mix two rankers, attribute the win by whose tracks get engaged with) or a switchback by session or day. Guard exposure, keep the exploration budget, and auto-roll back if skip or remove rates spike. The stored explanation plus your own reaction is evidence no metric supplies. candidate

Ground-truth circularity — the hardest honest problem

Three nested circuits validate a capability against a signal the capability itself produced. One: the period labels are validated against the vectors that defined them — near-tautological. Two: the sonic era is validated against the year tag it exists to replace, itself declared “poisoned” by remasters. Three: the audio→construct map is validated only by feedback the map elicited; propensity fixes evaluation bias, not the causal confound, and the excluded tail is never validated, so a wrong threshold can persist forever. unverified

The consequence, carried: where no independent ground truth exists — and with a single user it may simply not — the capability must be labelled unverifiable, not merely unproven. That covers the sonic-period model, the sonic-era estimate, and every proxy in the audio→construct map, including the learned mood, focus and energy heads. It is a limit of the evidence, not a defect of design: surface it as confidence, never as fact. unverified

The cost model is wrong; this is the corrected arithmetic

The sampling tension is the part the sources never name. The 30–60 s window that buys the compute back degrades exactly the metrics the hard cases need. Decide per metric. May be sampled: mean loudness, most timbral and spectral descriptors, coarse embeddings. May not: loudness range, crest factor, the production era cue, structure and segmentation, and microtiming over a grid — all need full tracks. Sampling them saves days and silently breaks the cases that motivate the system.

Chapter 09

Build plan and open decisions

The research phase is over. Chapters 2 to 8 argued what the science supports, where the design breaks, and what survives — almost none of it run on your 45,739 files. This is the commit point: what to build, in what order, and what only you decide. The sequence is candidate; the mechanisms are observed.

The sequencing principle

SynAmp's roadmap sequences correctly already: get off streaming subscriptions first, then build what exists nowhere else. This brain work belongs in the differentiator tier — Phase 3 — behind the Phase 2 playlist system. Three foundations are cheap now, painful to retrofit, so land them early:

  • A server-owned session and queue model. Plays, position and skip events belong on the server, not the browser; retrofitting that later is a rewrite.
  • "A playlist is a saved live query." The store must re-resolve membership, not replay a frozen list. The skeleton exists: the Folder | Playlist | Rollup tree with merge, shuffle, interleave and weighted modes in apps/brain.
  • A resumable, incremental analysis queue. Also sketched: the analyzer's enqueue_missing / claim / complete / fail interface and stages_done resume. Never let the first pass be a one-shot script.

Get these right and later phases are additive; get them wrong and Phase 3 is a migration.

The phased build plan

The decisions only you can make

The first week

  1. Extend the per-track record — services/analyzer/models.py: add the objective fields (production, structure, pulse, spectral) to AnalysisResult.
  2. Add invalidation keys — services/analyzer/queue.py and the store: store_version/index_version per job, bumped on a model change; stages_done is the resume point.
  3. Write the query-object schema — new apps/brain/src/schema/query-plan.ts: the Chapter 6 schema as a type, a validator, the plan hash.
  4. Save the plan, not the list — apps/brain persistence: each playlist keeps its plan and versions; /resolve recomputes.
  5. Create the exposure/feedback log — a brain DB migration: one row per (plan, track, position, event), written before anything reads it.
  6. Calibrate on a small subset — the cheap block over one album arc plus a few instrumental tracks, before the full run.
  7. Wire virtualised rendering — apps/web: point the playlist tree at a virtualised list before it grows past a few thousand rows.
  8. Unit-test the negation compiler — assert "instrumental, no piano" encodes without "piano".

How to know it is working

  • The strict focus prompt returns only instrumental, piano-free, above-tempo tracks and reports excluded counts.
  • A project across one artist's arc ranks that period above the artist's others.
  • Excluding a genre by sound removes tracks with no genre tag.
  • A thumbs-down changes the next result, and the explanation says why.
  • New files appear in a saved playlist without a refresh.

The bottom line. The research is solid, the mechanisms real, the pipeline coherent; the fix list is engineering, not rethinking. But the accuracy of the mood, era and energy signals is unproven on this particular library. Build the measurement in — confidence labels, excluded counts, the exposure log, calibrated thresholds — rather than assuming the signals are right. Assume nothing; instrument everything.
